Dissecando o papel do imunometabolismo na formação do
granuloma na sarcoidose
RESUMO
A sarcoidose é uma condição granulomatosa de etiologia desconhecida que afeta predominantemente
os pulmões. Este distúrbio é identificado pela formação de granulomas resultantes da acumulação de
células imunes e da resolução inadequada da inflamação. O metabolismo celular é um mecanismo
crucial pelo qual as células imunes modulam sua função. A indoleamina 2,3-dioxigenase 1 (IDO1) é a
enzima limitante no metabolismo do triptofano e apresenta um papel imunorregulador fundamental,
crucial na interface entre a inflamação e a imunidade celular. Os nossos dados mostraram que, entre os
genes envolvidos no metabolismo celular, a expressão do gene Ido1 é regulada positivamente durante a
formação de granulomas em modelos experimentais. Já os produtos da oxidação do L-triptofano pela
IDO1, conhecidos como L-quinureninas, têm sido descritos como detentores de funções tolerogénicas,
como ativação de células T reguladoras e produção de citocinas anti-inflamatórias. A nossa hipótese é
que a IDO1 representa um elemento essencial na patogénese da sarcoidose, regulando a mobilização
de células imunes e a formação de granulomas e, consequentemente, influenciando a progressão e o
desfecho da doença. Neste sentido, recorremos a um modelo in vivo de formação de granuloma usando
ratinhos selvagens e deficientes em Ido1. O nosso trabalho demonstra que a ausência do gene Ido1 em
ratinhos perturba a fase inicial da formação do granuloma, assim como a produção de citocinas e a
distribuição de células imunes. Em humanos, mostramos que a variante genética rs7820268 associada
à deficiência de IDO1 pode ditar o curso da doença. Estes resultados contribuem para o nosso
conhecimento dos mecanismos moleculares na base da inflamação granulomatosa e para o possível
desenvolvimento de novas intervenções terapêuticas para a sarcoidose.

Dissecting the role of immunometabolism in granuloma
formation in sarcoidosis
ABSTRACT
Sarcoidosis is a granulomatous condition with unknown etiology that predominantly affects the lungs.
This disorder is characterized by the formation of granulomas resulting from the accumulation of immune
cells and an inadequate resolution of inflammation. Cell metabolism is a crucial mechanism whereby
immune cells modulate their function. Indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1) is the rate-limiting enzyme
of tryptophan metabolism and displays a fundamental immunoregulatory role, crucial at the interface
between inflammation and cell immunity. Our data revealed that, among the genes involved in cellular
metabolism, expression of Ido1 gene was upregulated during granuloma formation in experimental
sarcoid granulomas. On the other hand, the products of L-tryptophan oxidation by IDO1, known as L-
kynurenines, have been described as having tolerogenic functions, such as T regulatory cell activation
and anti-inflammatory cytokine production. We hypothesize that IDO1 represents an essential modulatory
element in sarcoidosis by regulating immune cell mobilization and granuloma formation and, as a result,
influences the progression and the outcome of the disease. In this work, we took advantage of an in vivo
model of sarcoid granuloma using wild-type and Ido1 knockout mice. Our work shows that Ido1 deficiency
disturbs the early phase of granuloma formation, as well as cytokine production and immune cell
distribution. In patients, we showed that the rs7820268 genetic variant that underlies IDO1 deficiency
may dictate the severity and the outcome of the disease. These results contribute to an improved
understanding of the molecular mechanisms that underlie granulomatous inflammation and to the
potential development of novel therapeutic interventions for sarcoidosis.

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 Sarcoidosis
Sarcoidosis is a systemic inflammatory condition of unknow cause. A multitude of organs can be impacted
by sarcoidosis, including skin, liver and heart, although it most frequently affects the lungs (i.e. in 90% of
cases) 1. This illness progresses from an asymptomatic state to a persistent, chronic inflammation with
increasing organ impairment in around one-third of patients 2. The formation and accumulation of
epithelioid noncaseating granulomas in the affected tissues is the distinguishing immunopathological
signature of sarcoidosis 3. Despite recent advances, the exact triggers and molecular signals that initiate
and control granulomatous inflammation are still largely unknown. Since the pathophysiological
mechanisms and its biomarkers are not clearly identified, viable therapeutic choices remain limited 4.
Notwithstanding, further developments in our understanding of sarcoidosis development and its disease-
specific mechanisms will be essential for developing clinically useful tools and innovative therapeutic
interventions. In this sense, a large effort has recently been put in the characterization of the molecular
mechanisms associated to sarcoidosis. In this regard, innate immunity features represent the connecting
element between the initiation, persistence, and resolution of sarcoid granulomas, whereas adaptive
immune responses to selected antigens coordinates the establishment and amplification, and as well as
the fine-tuning, of granulomatous inflammation 5.
1.1.1 Epidemiology and etiology of sarcoidosis
Despite large disparities in the occurrence of sarcoidosis, epidemiological studies have contributed
considerably to outline the worldwide dispersion of the disease, evaluating long-term effects and detecting
potential risk factors. Depending on the geographical region, the prevalence of sarcoidosis varies widely,
ranging from 1–5 per 100,000 in South Korea, Taiwan and Japan, to 140–160 per 100,000 in Sweden
and Canada 6, 7. However, comparisons between regions are challenging since estimates are derived from
countless sources and are used in multiple settings. Additionally, data is lacking about multiple regions
around the world. The actual disease burden may be inflated, especially in circumstances where people
continue to be categorized as cases decades after the condition has resolved. Besides that, some surveys
only use information from hospitalizations, which underestimates the exact number of sarcoidosis
patients by excluding individuals who are only observed during outpatient treatment 8.
Sarcoidosis incidence in Sweden is 11.5 per 100,000 according to statistics from the Swedish
national register 9, which is higher than estimates from the United States (around 8-11 per 100,000) 10, 11
and Canada (6.8 per 100,000 per year) 12. The latest data from East Asia, corresponding to South Korea
reports, shows lower incidence levels (from 0.5 to 1.3 per 100,000) 13, 14. Once more, data sources,
2

diagnosis and clinical patterns in different regions might result in an unreliable estimation of incidence
and prevalence 7.
Knowing that patient populations are heterogeneous and might result in variable incidence and
prevalence estimates, race is a critical determinant for defining the rates of sarcoidosis. In fact, non-
Hispanic Black Americans have the highest rates of sarcoidosis in the United States with higher age-
adjusted death rates attributable to sarcoidosis, in comparison with non-Hispanic White Americans 15, 16, 17.
Particularly, Black American women exhibit the greatest levels 18. In fact, some reports claim that women
are more likely to suffer from sarcoidosis than males, presenting worse outcomes 19, 20.
In Portugal, a cohort with 409 patients from the Northern region was used to investigate the
epidemiological and clinical aspects of the impacted population 21. According to this study, the condition
peaks at about 40 years of age, with women accounting for 58.9% of all cases. Additionally, thoracic
involvement was observed in 94.8% of the patients. Intriguingly, even though the origin of sarcoidosis is
still unknown, the fact that the lung is the organ most commonly affected suggests that exposure to
airborne environmental antigens may be a potential cause for the disease 22.
It is plausible that several genetic and environmental factors contribute to sarcoidosis.
Granulomas are considered to form in the context of immune dysregulation through a multitude of
different processes. Sarcoidosis has a wide range of possible causes, including self-antigens such as
vimentin 23 and organic and inorganic particles 24, 25, 26. Multiple mycobacterial pathogen-associated
molecular patterns (PAMPs), such as mycobacterial catalase-peroxidase (mKatG) and superoxide
dismutase A (sodA), have been found in tissue and bronchoalveolar-lavage fluid (BALF) from sarcoidosis
patients 27, 28. Additionally, in different mouse models of pulmonary sarcoid inflammation, these
mycobacterial antigens induce granulomas that seem to be histologically and immunologically equivalent
to those found in human disease 29. Even so, not every sarcoidosis patient shows signs of mycobacterial
remains, which has driven researchers to speculate that different microbiological pathogens contribute
to the onset of sarcoidosis. In accordance, DNA and other molecules from Propionibacterium species
have been extracted from sarcoid granulomas, while most patients hold a distinctive adaptive immune
response to these associated antigens 30, 31. It has also been reported that Propionibacterium acnes causes
antigen-driven granulomatous inflammation in a variety of in vitro and in vivo models 32. Recently, it was
also discovered that antigens derived from the nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD)-dependent
histone deacetylase hst4 (NDPD) of the fungus Aspergillus nidulans were recognized by CD4+ T cells in
patients with acute pulmonary sarcoidosis 33, suggesting another causal trigger in the etiology of
sarcoidosis.
3

There is substantial evidence that a genetic predisposition exists due to the varying occurrence
and pathophysiology of sarcoidosis across multiple races and examples of familiar transmission of
disease 15, 34. Now more than ever, genetic studies have been focusing on immune-related genes that
potentially confer disease susceptibility 35, 36. As such, human leukocyte antigens (HLA), with functions in
the regulation of expression of the major histocompatibility complex (MHC), which is crucial for antigen
presentation and the regulation of adaptive immune responses, was proven to be associated with
sarcoidosis susceptibility, particularly in the class II region 37, 38. Considering every factor, and even if the
etiology of sarcoidosis is still unclear, it is currently believed that host genetics and environmental factors
play a concerted action to promote disease pathology 39.
1.1.2 Diagnosis and treatment
The absence of adequate biomarkers for the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of sarcoidosis
complicates therapeutic interventions. It is indeed challenging to diagnose sarcoidosis since it mirrors a
variety of health conditions such as cancer or inflammatory diseases.
Sarcoidosis diagnosis is based on a multimodality approach combining clinical, radiological, and
BAL analysis, supported by histological evaluation demonstrating the presence of non-caseating
granulomas, according to the American Thoracic Society/European Respiratory Society/World
Association for Sarcoidosis and Other Granulomatous Disorders statement 40, 41. In patients without
evidence of granulomas, diagnosis also involves clinical and radiological features associated with a BAL
CD4+/CD8+ lymphocyte ratio >3.5 42. Sarcoidosis is defined as acute if resolved within 2 years, either self-
limiting or following treatment, whereas chronic sarcoidosis referred to persistent disease beyond 2 years.
Despite numerous investigations, there is still no accurate and clinically used diagnostic
biomarker for sarcoidosis 43. Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) values above the norm can validate
the diagnosis but are not unique towards sarcoidosis 44. For some individuals, the diagnosis might not be
determined for months or even years due to it being dependent on the clinical manifestation, presence
of granulomas and exclusion of alternative causes. Moreover, in particular cases, sarcoidosis can be
diagnosed, but eventually manifestations are proven to be related to another condition 45.
Up to 50% of sarcoidosis patients will need therapy, a large fraction of patients will experience
spontaneous illness remission, and 10 to 30% of patients may experience chronic, persisting disease
with, in some cases, noticeable lung fibrosis and variable degrees of respiratory failure 46, 47, 48, 49. Indeed,
5% of sarcoidosis patients will die from the condition, with increased death rates observed in those with
respiratory failure, fibrotic pulmonary disease, pulmonary hypertension, and cardiac sarcoidosis 16, 50, 51, 52,
4

53.
Corticosteroids are the standard therapy for sarcoidosis, yet they are associated with decreased
health-related quality of life (HRQoL), morbidity, toxicity and several health complications, such as obesity
or endocrinal and cardiovascular side effects 54, 55, 56 (Figure 1). In this sense, steroid-sparing non-biologic
immunosuppressive drugs take place as “second-line agents” for treatment 57. The preferred steroid-
sparing non-biologic agent is methotrexate since it allows patients to reduce or even discontinue
corticosteroid therapy with positive outcomes 46, 47, 48, 58, 59. When individuals are intolerant or do not react to
first and second-line therapies, biological therapy implementation has promising results towards
sarcoidosis treatment 46. Tumor necrosis factor (TNF) inhibitors, considered “third-line agents”, are
administered in individuals with uncontrolled disease or those who experience toxicity upon “second-line”
treatments 46, 48, 60. Infliximab, the most used TNF inhibitor, has shown positive outcomes on severe forms
of sarcoidosis 61, 62. Despite their proven success with treating persistent sarcoidosis, these patients display
instead a higher probability for tuberculosis 63.
Figure 1. Available lines of therapy for sarcoidosis. The treatment of sarcoidosis is composed by three different lines
of therapy, with increasing specificity. The first line of therapy is a standard and unspecific treatment, mainly consisting in
corticosteroid administration. The second line of therapy is the one applied when corticosteroid intolerance appears and
englobes immunosuppressive drugs, such as methotrexate. Finally, the third line of therapy is applied to individuals with
uncontrolled disease or lack of response to other therapies, consisting in a biological therapy, such as infliximab, a TNF
inhibitor.
Genome-wide expression profiling has received attention as a potential tool for the diagnosis and
5

prognosis of diverse lung conditions 64. There are now more opportunities to find therapeutically
meaningful biomarkers because of incorporation of transcriptomic profiling in the blood and pulmonary
tissues of sarcoidosis patients. The genomic landscape thus distinguishes sarcoidosis from other
alternative diagnostics and provides a perspective into critical regulatory processes underpinning sarcoid
inflammation 65. Furthermore, the investigation of novel biomarkers and further establishment of the
groundwork for the implementation of personalized treatment in clinical settings may initiate with a deeper
understanding of the genetic background of sarcoidosis.
1.1.3 Histopathologic characteristics
The immunopathogenesis of sarcoidosis is focused on the growth and accumulation of non-caseating
epithelioid granulomas 66. Sarcoid granulomas are compact and structured concentrations of
macrophages that originate in response to a sustained immunological insult. Epithelioid and giant cells,
neutrophils, dendritic cells, B and T lymphocytes, and fibroblasts are only a few of the many types of cells
that compose mature granulomas 67. Yet, granulomas may still form in severe combined
immunodeficiency (SCID) mice, which essentially lack both B and T cells 68. This suggests that the adaptive
immune system plays a non-essential role in granuloma formation. Innate immunity therefore drives the
establishment and development of granulomatous lesions, while adaptive immunity contributes to
restrict and minimize the dissemination of the causative antigen.
Granuloma formation and development
The first step in the establishment of sarcoid granulomas is the activation of antigen-presenting cells
(APCs), which primarily consist of lung-resident macrophages activated by the recognition of poorly
soluble or insoluble antigens 69 (Figure 2). APCs activate the adaptive immune system by providing the
processed peptides to recruited CD4+ T lymphocytes after antigen phagocytosis. After detecting MHC-II
on the surface of lung-resident macrophages, T cells promote cytokine release that supports the
inflammatory environment firstly created by interactions between the insult and innate immune cells 70.
In addition, CD4+ T cells differentiate into type 1 effector T-helper (Th1) cells and promote survival and
activation of effector macrophages by releasing interferon-γ (IFN-γ) and TNF 71. Cytokine-activated
macrophages also produce TNF, which boosts cellular immune responses important for granuloma
maturation. In fact, individuals with a progressive disease status present higher TNF values than those in
a remissive state 72.
6

Figure 2. An outline of the development of sarcoid granulomas. (1) The immune system identifies persistent antigenic
stimuli through antigen-presenting cells (APCs), such as dendritic cells or lung-resident macrophages. (2) After migrating to
lymph nodes, APCs present the antigens that had previously been phagocytized and, upon MHC-II cross-presentation, activate
T cells. (3) By releasing cytokines, T cell activation promotes an inflammatory environment that recruits and activates other
immune cells. (4) The induction of an inflammatory environment amplifies the production of cytokines and chemokines to
further boost cellular immune responses. (5) Under normal circumstances, the immune system effectively eliminates the
threat. However, when the immune system is unable to eradicate it, immune cells begin to accumulate in the lung. A
mesenchymal-epithelial remodeling process occurs in activated macrophages, giving rise to epithelioid and giant multinuclear
cells. Other immune cells, such as T and B cells, build up on the surroundings, and uncontrolled inflammation leads
to fibroblast accumulation on the granuloma, developing pulmonary fibrosis.
When incapable of eliminating persistent antigens, macrophages undergo structural and
molecular changes that attracts other immune cells, leading to immune cell accumulation and,
consequently, granuloma formation 73. In particular, activated macrophages enter into an epithelioid state
and start to attach to adjacent cells by cell membrane connections until a point they may completely fuse
their membranes and form multinucleated giant cells 74. These morphological modifications have been
detailed in the context of tuberculosis 75. Macrophages suffer a mesenchymal-epithelial remodeling and
display classical epithelial markers such E-cadherin in the development of adherent junctions during
macrophage merging, giving rise to epithelioid and giant multinuclear cells. By inversely regulating
macrophage quiescence, the mammalian target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) has recently been
shown to be a crucial rheostat of the inflammation associated with sarcoidosis 76. The conversion of
7

murine macrophages into hypertrophic epithelioid cells that spontaneously form granulomas is thus
facilitated by the mTORC1 chronic activation as the result of the genetic deletion of the TSC2 negative
regulator.
Granuloma formation and maintenance is instigated by a combination of chemokines that
promotes the recruitment of circulating cells, such as patrolling monocytes and macrophages,
neutrophils, and lymphocytes, to the lung tissue 77. While Th1-dominant environments are often restricted
to disease sites, T helper type 17 (Th17) cells have been linked to the etiology of sarcoidosis. Intriguingly,
the percentage of IFN-γ-producing Th17.1 cells is increased in the peripheral blood and BAL from
sarcoidosis patients when compared to healthy controls and is associated with disease course 78. In
addition, evaluation of pulmonary and mediastinal lymph node biopsies identified infiltrates of IL-17-
producing T cells in the sarcoid area around the center of the granuloma 79. The pathogenic effects of
Th17 effector cells in sarcoidosis are still unclear, their relevance in the immune response is mostly
attributed to their capacity to direct the mobilization of neutrophils to the inflamed tissue 80.
Even though most granulomas may remit with favorable long-term results, the primary cause of
morbidity and death in sarcoidosis is the development of pulmonary fibrosis, being a prevalent side effect
for an increasing percentage of patients who suffer from persistent and uncontrolled inflammation 51. The
capacity to maintain and regenerate host tissue becomes the dominant survival strategy after immune
responses enter the chronic phase 81. Yet, a persistent fibrotic scar may develop by an excessive buildup
of extracellular matrix components as a result of repair in the areas with dysregulated inflammation. The
mechanisms underlying lung fibrosis remain unclear, however numerous research studies back up the
idea that a Th2-polarized program firmly counters the dominant Th1 responses, in which IFN-γ inhibits
fibroblast proliferation and extracellular matrix deposition 82. In fact, matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs)
and tissue inhibitor of matrix metalloproteinases (TIMPs) expression are regulated by IFN-γ, which control
collagen degradation and production, respectively. Likewise, epithelioid and giant cells in sarcoid
granulomas were shown to express MMP-2, MMP-9, and their inhibitors TIMP-2 and TIMP-1 83.
Nevertheless, it is unclear how these cells may be related to pulmonary fibrosis. Recruited fibroblasts
form a collagen matrix in response to Th2 pro-fibrotic mediators (IL-4 and IL-13), as well as other cytokines
generated by granuloma resident cells, such as transforming growth factor (TGF)-β1 and surround the
sarcoid granuloma, which eventually results in organ failure 84.
1.2 Immunometabolism in sarcoidosis
In inflammatory conditions, the metabolic processes are critical for the immune response. One of the
8

most relevant examples regards the mTORC1 pathway, whose chronic activation is linked to the
development of sarcoidosis 76. Due to the expression of degradative enzymes, the epithelial transformation
of macrophages and their metabolic shift via mTORC1 towards enhanced glycolysis fosters their
proliferative, microbicidal, and secretory ability to eliminate large or less degraded foreign bodies 74. The
immune response is eventually concentrated locally when these cells clump to form an early granuloma.
In a mouse model of sarcoidosis, the granulomas of animals deficient for the long pentraxin 3 (PTX3), a
central component of humoral innate immunity, displayed higher levels of phosphorylated S6K1 kinase,
a downstream target of mTORC1, when compared to wild-type animals 85. Similarly, everolimus-mediated
mTORC1 inhibition restrained granuloma development in Ptx3 KO mice 85. Ultimately, PTX3 deficiency
elicits mTORC1 activation via the complement component C5a and the downstream metabolic
reprogramming of macrophages towards enhanced glycolysis, promoting their proliferation and
aggregation 85.
Although there is growing evidence towards the role of immune cell metabolism in sarcoid
granuloma formation, the exact metabolic mechanisms and the way they crosstalk to foster granuloma
formation remain mostly elusive. Amino acids are constituents of proteins that modulate cellular
responses through signaling or that act as precursors for energy-producing pathways. For example,
individuals with active tuberculosis (TB) were found to have lower levels of L-tryptophan (L-TRP) and
increased amounts of its metabolites 86, 87, 88. Of note, 11 out of 20 serum metabolites with the potential
to distinguish between patients with active tuberculosis and healthy controls were amino acids
or derivatives 86. Importantly, sarcoidosis and tuberculosis share similar clinical patterns to a large extent
89 and, as such, it is plausible that the molecular mechanisms underlying such disorders are analogous.
1.2.1 Tryptophan metabolism
IDO1 is a heme-containing enzyme that catalyzes the first and rate-limiting step of the kynurenine (KYN)
pathway. The essential amino acid L-TRP is depleted as a result of the enzymatic conversion of L-TRP
into N-formylkynurenine, a precursor of the kynurenines. The KYN pathway is the second primary source
of NAD+, a reducing equivalent that is required for many metabolic reactions, such as glycolysis and the
tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle 90. In fact, IDO1-driven catabolism of L-TRP can result in the endogenous
synthesis of NAD+ through the KYN pathway 91, 92. This well described catalytic function is complemented
with the recently identified signaling function of the protein itself. IDO1 is mainly composed by two
domains: a large domain containing the catalytic cleft and a small domain with two function
immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motifs (ITIMs), ITIM1 and ITIM2, linked to the noncatalytic
9

signaling functions of the enzyme 93.
Even though it is detected in basal conditions, IDO1 is an inducible enzyme, mainly induced by
IFN-γ as a part of a conserved mechanism to prevent harmful inflammatory responses 93, 94. Moreover,
IDO1 can be induced in the long-term in a TGF-β environment. Tyrosine phosphorylation of the ITIM
sequence allows it to function as a docking site for molecular patterns with Src homology 2 (SH2)
domains. As a matter of fact, TGF-β promotes Src homology domain phosphatase-1 (SHP1) and SHP2
upregulation and favors interaction with ITIM1 over ITIM2 95, 96. Then, IDO1-SHP1-SHP2 complex formation
promotes activation of the anti-inflammatory non-canonical factor nuclear kappa B (NF-κB) pathway and
induces a sustained Ido1 expression 95.
An immunosuppressive microenvironment caused by KYN production and L-TRP depletion has
been implicated in IDO1-mediated inhibitory effect on T cells and increased regulatory T (Treg) cell
differentiation 97, 98. The lack of L-TRP causes the general control nonderepressible 2 (GCN2) gene to
become active, which inhibits the expression of a number of genes, including the one that encodes the
pro-inflammatory cytokine interleukin-6 (IL-6) 99. This suppresses the transition of Treg cells into
proinflammatory Th17 cells 100, 101. Furthermore, L-KYN is an endogenous aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR)
agonist that can support the differentiation of effector T cells into Treg cells and the induction of IDO1 to
modulate immune activity 102, 103.
IDO1 is not the sole enzyme capable of converting L-TRP into L-KYN. IDO2 and tryptophan 2,3-
dioxygenase (TDO) are two other enzymes that can accomplish the same purpose. IDO2 is an isoform of
IDO1, both of which are encoded by genes located in chromosome 8 and both share high degree of
amino acid sequence similarity 104. Epithelial, vascular endothelial, neoplastic, and antigen-presenting cells
generally express both isoforms 92, 105, with IDO2 being preferentially expressed in the liver under
physiological circumstances 106. However, IDO2 exhibits abnormalities on its catalytic activity and its
depletion does not result in decreased L-KYN levels 107. In fact, further research is needed to fully
understand the physiological significance of IDO2 since its function is yet unknown. On the other hand,
TDO has a more well-defined purpose, being located in the liver and showing high substrate specificity,
in comparison to the promiscuous profile of IDO1, which can bind to various indole-bearing compounds,
such as serotonin, melatonin or tryptamine 108. TDO degrades excess dietary L-TRP, but it is believed that
L-KYN are also further degraded in the liver since the systemic levels are maintained by IDO1 activity 109,
110. Of the enzymes that metabolize L-TRP through the KYN pathway, only IDO1 and IDO2 are present and
active in immune cells.
10

IDO1 in infectious diseases
The L-TRP metabolism is an essential modulator of immunological host-pathogen interactions. By
functioning as Toll-like receptor (TLR) ligands, a number of PAMPs can increase IDO1 expression 111, 112.
Furthermore, L-TRP malnutrition has the power to rewire certain auxotroph invaders, as seen with
Mycobacterium tuberculosis, enabling them to regain the ability to generate this crucial amino acid 113. In
addition, the production of kynurenines by IDO1 in infectious illnesses creates an immunosuppressive
state that may hamper the clearance of the pathogen. The suppression of IDO1 activity in macaques with
active TB decreases the burden of M. tuberculosis, improves animal survival, and reduces pathology 114.
Whether enhanced IDO1 is a cause of the progression into active infection or a coping mechanism against
the microorganism is still unknown at this point. These findings, however, imply that there may be a
chance for IDO1 inhibitors to be used as a successful antimicrobial treatment. In addition to controlling
the pathogen burden, IDO1 activation may restrain the pathogenic immune response that could
exacerbate the condition. Since the innate and adaptive immune responses to the fungus are dysregulated
in experimental fungal infections, IDO1 inhibition significantly worsens the illness and the concomitant
inflammatory pathology 115. It is noteworthy that fungi express IDO1 and synthesize kynurenines that are
important for both fungal and host viability 116.
IDO1 in autoimmunity
IDO1-mediated immunomodulatory processes play a significant role in the pathophysiology of
autoimmune diseases characterized by an overactive immune response to self-antigens. In autoimmunity,
the overactivation of effector immune cells is achieved as the result of the diminished activity of IDO1,
which under healthy settings may suppress effector T cells and promote Treg lymphocytes in an antigen-
specific way 117, 118, 119. In peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) obtained from pediatric type 1
diabetes (T1D) patients 120 or dendritic cells (DCs) from nonobese diabetic mice 121, 122, 123, IDO1 activity was
found to be impaired. Poor IDO1 activity in DCs from non-obese diabetic (NOD) mice and PBMCs from
diabetic children is a result of a fast turnover of the protein brought on by an IL-6-driven pro-inflammatory
environment. In fact, inhibition of the IL-6 receptor (IL-6R) through the administration of tocilizumab (TCZ)
can be used to rescue IDO1 deficiency 90. Through an IDO1-dependent mechanism, IL-6 inhibition
prevents IDO1 degradation and improves normoglycemia in diabetic NOD mice 124. In PBMCs from a
subpopulation of T1D children with a deregulated IL-6R-supressor of cytokine signaling 3 (SOCS3) axis
and a distinctive frequency of the single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) rs7820268 in the IDO1 gene,
TCZ also restored L-TRP metabolism 120. Although various SNPs in IDO1 have been linked to the
development of autoimmune and chronic inflammatory illnesses, such as T1D, Crohn's disease, systemic
11

sclerosis, and multiple sclerosis, the majority of them impact intronic areas or the IDO1 promoter and
lack a cause-and-effect connection 93. The absence of a genotype-to-protein connection represents a
barrier to the exploitation of IDO1 as a predictive biomarker and viable therapeutic target. All this supports
the idea that IDO1 should be developed as a biomarker that predicts outcomes and helps with precision
therapy in the area of autoimmunity. Likewise, IDO1 could have a huge impact on the course of
sarcoidosis. In fact, numerous medical signs of sarcoidosis are similar to other typical autoimmune
disorders 125. Our hypothesis relied thus on the possible influence that IDO1 deficiency and the rs7820268
SNP may have on the progression and outcome of this particular granulomatous disease.
12

2. OBJECTIVES
13

Despite the significance of sarcoid granulomas for the pathogenesis of the disease, our molecular
understanding of the signals underpinning macrophage transition into hypertrophic cells that aggregate,
initiate, and maintain granulomatous inflammation remains elusive. Therefore, an improved knowledge
about these processes is ultimately required for the design of rationale therapies targeting pathogenetic
mechanisms. Since IDO1 exhibits a critical immunoregulatory activity at the crossroads between
inflammation and immunity, we hypothesize that a functional dysregulation of IDO1 in sarcoidosis could
be a pivotal molecular hallmark linked to granuloma formation and affecting disease progression and
outcome. Therefore, we aim to:
1. Describe the role of IDO1 in the development and resolution of sarcoid granulomas.
2. Define the role of IDO1 deficiency in immune cell recruitment and function during granulomatous
inflammation.
3. Assess how IDO1 deficiency impacts susceptibility and traits of human sarcoidosis.
By unraveling the processes underlying sarcoid granuloma formation and maintenance, we expect to
contribute to the development of novel and personalized therapeutic strategies for efficient clinical
interventions in sarcoidosis.

3. MATERIAL AND METHODS
15

3.1 Mouse strains
C57BL/6 Ido1+/+ (WT) and C57BL/6 Ido1-/- (KO) (B6.129-Ido1tm1Alm/J) mice were bred under specific-
pathogen-free (SPF) conditions and kept at the Life and Health Sciences Research Institute (ICVS) Animal
Facility. Mice were fed ad libitum with standard solid food and water and were kept at light/dark cycles
of 12 hours, temperature of 18-25°C and 40-60% humidity. All the procedures were approved by the
Ethics Committee for Research in Life and Health Sciences of the University of Minho (SECVS 074/2016),
according to the guidelines sanctioned by the Ethical Committee for Animal Experiments and Direção-
Geral de Alimentação e Veterinária (DGAV). All procedures followed the EU adopted regulations (Directive
2010/63/EU).
3.2 NDPD-Sepharose 4B beads coupling by cyanogen bromide-activation
Briefly, 0.5 g of Sepharose® 4B beads (Sigma-Aldrich, Missouri, USA) activated with cyanogen bromide
were washed and swollen in cold 1 mM HCl for 30 minutes. A total of 100 mL was added in numerous
aliquots, with the supernatant being gently filtered off in between additions. The resin was then washed
twice, one with 35 mL of 0.1 M NaHCO buffer containing 0.5 M NaCl (pH 8.3-8.5) and another with 750
3
mL of distilled water (coupling buffer). The resin was transferred to a solution containing 8 mg of the
Aspergillus nidulans ligand NDPD peptide (RLGSDNRLTRL) 33 in coupling buffer (GenScript, New Jersey,
USA), and gently mixed overnight at 4°C. Following incubation, the unreacted ligands were discarded by
washing the beads three times in 50 mL of coupling buffer and centrifuging for 10 minutes at room
temperature at 10,000 rpm. Using 0.2 M glycine (pH 8.0), the unreacted chemical groups were blocked
for two hours at room temperature. Thereafter, the beads were thoroughly washed to remove the blocking
solution. This was performed over four cycles, each cycle alternating between acid and basic solutions,
first with 25 mL of basic coupling buffer and then with 25 mL of 0.1 M acetate buffer combined with 0.5
M NaCl (pH 4.0).
3.3 Murine model of lung sarcoid granuloma formation
Lung sarcoid granulomas were induced as previously described, with minor adjustments 29, 85. A
subcutaneous injection of 20 µg of the NDPD-peptide, together with 0,25 mL of Freund's incomplete
adjuvant, was used to sensitize eight-week-old Ido1 WT and KO mice on day -14. Fourteen days later,
6,000 NDPD-coupled Sepharose 4B beads in 0.4 mL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) (Thermo Fisher,
USA) were injected into the tail vein of mice. Mice were euthanized using a lethal dose of anesthesia,
following cardiac perfusion, two, four- and seven-days after bead administration (Figure 3).

3.4 Histological and morphometric analysis of lung granulomas
Mice had their lungs removed after being perfused with PBS 1x. The left lobe was isolated and fixed with
formaldehyde solution 3.7% (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) for at least 48h, before processing and paraffin
embedding. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was performed to allow histological evaluation and
granuloma size measurement in lung paraffin-sections with a 5 µm of thickness. The area of granulomas
was quantified using ImageJ, version 1.53d, after acquisition of images with an Olympus BX61 widefield
upright microscope using a DP70 high resolution camera (Olympus, Japan). The measurements were
performed on a minimum of 20 granulomas per lung section, on a total of 3 sections per animal,
sectioned through the central NDPD-coupled bead nidus.
3.5 Granuloma microdissection and RNA sequencing
After mice were euthanized, their entire lungs were removed, submerged in Optimal Cutting Temperature
(OCT) compound in a ratio of 1:2 of PBS and rapidly frozen at -80⁰C. Afterwards, the tissue was cut with
10 µm thickness and stained with 1% cresyl violet. Granulomas on the tissue were identified and isolated
17

using laser-capture microdissection technology in order to isolate granuloma RNA. Sample processing,
sequencing and analysis was performed at IMGM Laboratories GmbH (Germany), as described 126.
3.6 Protein quantification
Mice lungs were excised and kept at -80⁰C. For protein quantification, Bradford dye-binding (Bio-Rad,
USA) method was used. Lysis buffer and protease inhibitor solution was prepared and used at a volume
of 800 µL per sample containing half of each adult lobe. The lung tissue was transferred to a tube
containing lysis buffer before disruption with up-and-down twisted movements. The lysate was
homogenized 15 times through 19-, 21- and 25-gauge needles attached to a syringe. After 15 minutes
on ice, the lysate was centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4⁰C. Afterwards, 20 µg of protein
were mixed with Laemmli buffer (Bio-Rad), and samples were then boiled, separated on a 12% SDS-PAGE
gel, and transferred to nitrocellulose membranes (Bio-Rad). The following primary antibodies were used
in Western blotting following the manufacturer’s instructions: both mouse anti-β-actin antibody (Abcam,
UK) and anti-IDO1 antibody in a 1:1000 dilution. Anti-mouse secondary antibody was diluted to 1:5000.
Thermo Chemoluminescence (SuperSignalTM West Femto Maximum Sensitivity Substrate; ThermoFisher
Scientific) was used to generate the blots, and the ChemiDocTM XRS system (Bio-Rad) was used to detect
the results. The ImageLab 4.1 analysis program (Bio-Rad) was used to calculate signal intensities.
3.7 Immunofluorescence imaging in paraffin-embedded lung sections
Paraffin was removed from lung sections in two washes of xylol (5 minutes each) and then washed two
times in ethanol 100%, ethanol 90%, ethanol 70% and water for 2 minutes each. The slides were boiled
for 15 minutes in 10 mM citrate buffer (pH 6.0) for antigen retrieval in a microwave oven. After slides
cooled down at room temperature, the non-specific antibody binding sites were blocked with 5% donkey
serum (Sigma-Aldrich) in PBS for 1 hour at room temperature. Slides were then incubated at 4°C
overnight with the primary antibodies Mac-2 (B2C10) 1:100, CD3 (RM0027-3B19) 1:100 (Santa Cruz,
USA) and Gr-1 (RB6-8C5) 1:100 (BD Biosciences, USA). On the next day, slides were stained with the
secondary biotinylated IgG (H+L) antibody 1:250 for 1 hour at room temperature. Afterwards, streptavidin-
conjugated Alexa Fluor 568 1:200 (Thermo Fisher) was added to the tissue sections. Nuclei were
counterstained for 10 minutes at room temperature with 0.1 g/mL DAPI. Slides were washed with PBS
containing 0.02% (v/v) Tween-20 after each stage. PermaFluor Aqueous Mounting Medium (Thermo
Fisher) was used to mount the slides, and images were captured on an Olympus FV1000 confocal
microscope. ImageJ 1.53d was used to quantify fluorescence.

3.8 Cytokine and chemokine quantification
Excised lungs were homogenized in 2 mL of PBS, centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 2 minutes at 4⁰C and
supernatants were stored at -80⁰C. Before measuring cytokines and chemokines, samples were

CXCL9 (MIG), CXCL10 (IP-10), CCL3 (MIP-1α), CCL4 (MIP-1β), CXCL13 (BLC), CXCL5 (LIX) and CCL22
(MDC), were profiled using the 13-plex LEGENDplex Mouse Proinflammatory Chemokine Panel
(BioLegend), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Samples were analyzed on a BD FACS LSRII
instrument (BD Biosciences) and processed using FlowJo v.10 (Tree Star Inc.).

4.1 Lung granulomatous inflammation induces the expression of IDO1 in vivo
We aimed to clarify the possible immunoregulatory role of IDO1 in the molecular events that control
sarcoid inflammation in vivo. Using the NDPD peptide covalently coupled to Sepharose beads to elicit an
antigen-driven immune response, we investigated the induction of IDO1 expression in WT mice exposed
to a well-established murine model of lung granulomatous inflammation 29. We found that Ido1 gene
expression was mostly observed in the granulomas of WT mice 2 days after embolization, to decrease
thereafter on day 4 (Figure 5A and B). In support of gene expression data, Western-Blot analysis of the
lung on day 2 following bead administration demonstrated a 1.8-fold up-regulation of IDO1, with a minor
decrease on day 4, analogous to gene expression (Figure 5C, D, and E). These findings collectively show
that IDO1 is induced during the formation of pulmonary granulomas and is a potential mediator of
sarcoidosis-related inflammation.

4.2 IDO1 regulates early granuloma formation
We subsequently aimed to define the effect of IDO1 deficiency on the susceptibility to sarcoidosis in vivo
23

by inducing experimental granulomatous lung inflammation in both WT and KO mice (Figure 6A). KO
animals form larger granulomas compared to their WT counterparts, as shown by histological and
morphometric analyses of lung sections 2, 4, and 7 days following NDPD-coupled bead embolization
(Figure 6B and C). This was particularly evident at earlier timepoints, most notably after 2 days post-
administration). At this time point, the mean size of the granulomas induced by the NDPD-coupled beads
was 12,496 ± 702 µm2 (mean ± SEM) and 24,774 ± 1,690 µm2 in the WT and KO groups, respectively.
This difference was attenuated at later timepoints (between day 4 and day 7), with granulomas in KO
mice showing similar mean sizes to WT mice. Considering the above, IDO1 appears to play a role in the
development of granulomas in an animal model of granulomatous inflammation.

consequences of IDO1 deficiency to the cellular dynamics during granuloma formation by analyzing the

4.5 The IDO1 rs7820268 SNP is associated with the development of sarcoidosis
In humans, the IDO1 rs7820268 SNP have been commonly associated with an increased susceptibility
to several inflammatory and autoimmune conditions, including type I diabetes 128. Along this line, to
evaluate whether human genetic deficiency of IDO1 might increase the susceptibility to sarcoidosis, we
genotyped 174 sarcoidosis patients and 447 healthy controls of European descent 120. By performing a
case-control genetic association study, we observed a higher occurrence of the CC genotype among
sarcoidosis patients in comparison with healthy controls (p value = 0.001581) (Figure 10A and B),
pinpointing this specific genotype as a risk-conferring factor for the developing of sarcoidosis.

4.6 IDO1 regulates leukocyte recruitment and disease progression in sarcoidosis
As already mentioned, we noticed an enhanced vulnerability to murine sarcoidosis together with an

5. DISCUSSION AND FINAL CONSIDERATIONS
31

The structure and function of granulomas have been the subject of a recent explosion of new information.
We have learned vital information on the causes and consequences of granuloma formation, and we have
started to understand the unique roles that each myeloid cell plays within the intricate network of
proinflammatory mediators during this dynamic process 70. Compared to the traditional infectious
granulomas in tuberculosis, our understanding of granulomatous disorders with unknown causes, such
as sarcoidosis, remains far behind 70, 90. The onset and maintenance of granulomatous inflammation in
sarcoidosis is in fact still poorly understood, despite the importance of granulomas in disease
pathophysiology 5. Even though the histopathological order of events that lead to granuloma formation is
well understood, genetic and molecular research on the signaling pathways that convert macrophages
into hypertrophic, epithelioid cells that cluster to initiate and maintain sarcoid granulomas is scarce 76.
Here, we describe IDO1 as a crucial regulatory node in the immunopathogenesis of sarcoidosis by
regulating key immunological pathways, including the recruitment of leukocytes to the lungs and the
development of granulomatous inflammation in vivo and in human sarcoidosis.
Both the innate and adaptive immune systems are considered to play a role in the formation and
shaping of sarcoid granulomas 73. According to growing evidence, metabolic processes play a crucial role
in the immunopathogenesis of sarcoidosis by helping to initiate and maintain granulomatous
inflammation 76, 85. Within the different metabolic pathways, IDO1, an amino acid metabolism-related
enzyme, has been shown to play a significant role in the immunopathogenesis of a variety of
granulomatous and autoimmune conditions 93. IDO1 has been recognized to tightly control immunological
responses by suppressing the inflammatory phenotypes of immune cells 103. In agreement, we discovered
that the expression of IDO1 is increased in the airways of mice exposed to NDPD peptide-coated beads
during the early stages of sarcoid granuloma formation, indicating that IDO1 is crucial for the development
of granulomas in sarcoidosis.
IDO1 is widely believed to have a regulatory role at the interface of inflammation and cell
immunity 130, but its function in granulomatous inflammation associated with sarcoidosis is still unclear.
Here, we show that in a mouse model of NDPD-induced experimental granulomatous inflammation,
typical signs of sarcoidosis such as the development of non-caseating granulomas and dysregulated
immunological parameters, were amplified. Our findings show that IDO1-deficient mice, particularly in
the early phases of granuloma development (day 2 post-embolization), produced considerably larger
granulomas when compared to their WT littermates. This initial intensification of inflammation detected
in KO mice was not accompanied by a sudden infiltration of immune cells into the lung parenchyma. It
is intriguing that a decline in the proportion of neutrophils in the granuloma was observed, raising the
32

question about the nature of the cells that are contributing to enlarging the granulomas. The existence of
dendritic cells (DCs) in the granuloma niche is one of the first unanswered concern. This may be important
since these immune cells are among the main cell types that express IDO1, in particular, tolerogenic DCs,
that lead to inhibition memory and effector T cells, and induction of Treg cells 118 that could be locally
accumulating. Our data fails to demonstrate an effect of IDO1 on the number of T cells according to their
subpopulations. Likewise, additional experiments are required to understand whether instead T cell
activation and function might be altered. Moreover, the number of T cells and the quality of their response
should also be analyzed at later timepoints. The presence of non-immune cells within the granuloma is
another possible explanation for the increased granuloma size. In fact, alveolar epithelial cells may
represent an important constituent of granulomas in IDO1 KO mice, even though these were found not
to differ between WT and PTX3 KO, the latter strain also associated with the formation of larger
granulomatous lesions 85.
A remarkable finding from our analysis regards the decreased neutrophil infiltration observed in
the granulomatous lesions from IDO1 KO mice. Although the role of neutrophils in sarcoidosis is not well
described yet, this decrease argues in favor of a protective role for neutrophils in restraining granuloma
assembly and maturation. The fact that CXCL1 and CXCL5, chemokines that act as potent neutrophil
chemoattractants are diminished further supports this hypothesis. However, the mechanisms that rely
on neutrophils through which these contribute to the control of granuloma formation remain to be
assessed.
The SNP rs7820268 in IDO1 has recently been demonstrated to affect IDO1 expression in a
variety of human tissues. In many autoimmune disorders, such as type 1 diabetes and multiple sclerosis,
this SNP has been linked to defective tryptophan catabolism. Additionally, this SNP has been linked to
CD8+ Treg cell dysfunction in systemic sclerosis 131. The precise signaling pathways by which this SNP
affects IDO1 expression and/or function are still unclear. We found that the CC genotype in this SNP
confers an increased risk for sarcoidosis. This genotype was previously identified to result in a loss-of-
function of IDO1 in type 1 diabetes 120, which may be the case also in sarcoidosis. This question should
be addressed by the quantification of tryptophan/kynurenine ratios, which allow the estimation of IDO1
activity. Furthermore, immune profiling of BAL samples revealed an enhanced leukocyte recruitment in
patients carrying the risk-conferring genotype, which may, in part, help explaining disease severity and its
clinical course. Moreover, to better understand the mechanisms whereby the IDO1 SNP might also
influence granuloma formation, it is possible to resort to in vitro human granuloma formation models
using PBMCs derived from patients with sarcoidosis 132.
33

As previously mentioned, IDO1 is an immunomodulator of inflammatory responses, and its
dysfunction can lead to an uncontrolled inflammatory environment and immune cell recruitment. This
raises questions concerning the importance of tight control of this particular enzyme, considering that the
exuberant enhancement of IDO1 can be just as harmful as its functional loss.
In conclusion, our findings demonstrate that IDO1, a crucial player in tryptophan metabolism, acts
as an immunoregulatory node during granulomatous inflammation in sarcoidosis in both an in vivo model
and in patients. This project has contributed with important insights to ongoing efforts in minimizing the
knowledge gap of the molecular and cellular players involved in granulomatous inflammation in
sarcoidosis. It is thus expected to shed light into new and tailored medical interventions leading to a more
successful management of this condition.
