Development of Quality Assessment Tools for Question Construction: Utilizing AI Models
in Detecting Flaws in Item Writing
ABSTRACT
Introduction: One of the most challenging area in medical education is assessment. Multiple choice
questions (MCQs) are one of the most used method of assessment in educational setting. However
their construction is time consuming, and the nature of MCQs architecture make it difficult to build
robust and reliable test items. The gold standard for quality control is the manual detection of these
flaws, however this process is time-consuming and costly. An effective automated tool would make this
process more friendly. New advances in artificial intelligence both in accessibility and ease of use, make
it a possible pathway to automatize this process. Therefore, his project aims to assess the feasibility
and efficacy of using Artificial Intelligence (AI) to detect item writing flaws (IWFs) in Multiple-Choice
Questions.
Methods: To evaluate GPT-4 we used a sample of 106 items taken from a database already screened
for IWFs. The native database consists of 1150 questions from the Portuguese National Selection Exam
(NSE) conducted between 2009 and 2018 and the pilot exam of the Residency National Access Exam
(RNAE) and was screened for IWFs using established criteria and quality ratings (Rush, 2016;
Jozefowicz, 2002) using manual identification. The automated analysis assisted by GPT-4 was
performed using prompt optimization for a group of specific flaws.
Results: Of the items analyzed, 98.1% had at least one flaw. The flaw identified with the highest
sensitivity was "negative stems" and "longest option is correct" items. GPT-4 had low sensitivity and
high rates of disagreement in the identification of the remaining items.
Conclusions: The AI model GPT-4 has intrinsic knowledge in construction and validation of multiple-
choice questions and construction flaws identification, however, it does perform worse than human
review.
Keywords: artificial intelligence, assessment, item writing flaw, multiple choice question.

Desenvolvimento de Ferramentas de Avaliação de Qualidade para Construção de
Questões: a utilização de Modelos de IA na Detecção de Falhas na construção de Itens
RESUMO
Introdução: Uma das áreas mais desafiadoras na educação médica é a avaliação. As pergunta de
escolha múltipla (PEM) são um dos métodos mais utilizados de avaliação em ambientes educacionais.
No entanto, a sua construção é demorada, e a natureza da arquitetura das PEM torna difícil construir
itens de teste robustos e confiáveis. O padrão de ouro para o controle de qualidade é a detecção
manual dessas falhas, no entanto, este processo é demorado e custoso. Uma ferramenta automatizada
eficaz tornaria este processo mais amigável. Novos avanços na inteligência artificial, tanto em
acessibilidade quanto em facilidade de uso, tornam possível um caminho para automatizar este
processo. Portanto, este projeto visa avaliar a viabilidade e eficácia do uso da Inteligência Artificial (IA)
para detectar falhas na elaboração de itens em PEM.
Métodos: Para avaliar o GPT-4, usamos uma amostra de 106 itens retirados de um banco de dados já
analisado para IWFs. O banco de dados original consiste em 1150 perguntas do Exame Nacional de
Seleção português realizado entre 2009 e 2018 e o exame piloto da Prova Nacional de Acesso e foi
analisado para IWFs usando critérios estabelecidos e classificações de qualidade (Rush, 2016;
Jozefowicz, 2002) utilizando identificação manual. A análise automatizada assistida pelo GPT-4 foi
realizada usando otimização de prompt para um grupo de falhas específicas.
Resultados: Dos itens analisados, 98,1% tinham pelo menos uma falha. A falha identificada com maior
sensibilidade foi em itens de "stems negativas" e "a opção mais longa é a correta". O GPT-4 teve baixa
sensibilidade e altas taxas de discordância na identificação dos demais itens.
Conclusões: O modelo de IA GPT-4 possui conhecimento intrínseco na construção e validação de
questões de múltipla escolha e identificação de falhas na construção, no entanto, apresenta
desempenho inferior à revisão humana.
Palavras-chave: avaliação, identificação de falhas, inteligência artificial, perguntas de escolha
múltipla.

INTRODUCTION
Assessment in Medical Education
Assessment in medical education has undergone significant evolution, moving from a narrow focus on
measurement aimed at distinguishing the competent from the not competent, towards a more
nuanced, integrated system perspective. Initially, assessments were primarily concerned with reliability
and construct validity, emphasizing reproducibility and minimizing human judgment to classify
individuals' competence straightforwardly. However, as understanding deepened, the field recognized
the limitations of such an approach and shifted towards incorporating human judgment within
workplace-based assessments, acknowledging the complexity of assessing competence in authentic
settings.
This evolution reflects a broader shift from viewing assessment as a mere measurement tool to
understanding it as a complex process that integrates human judgment, supported by expertise and
robust assessment methodologies. Currently, the paradigm treats assessment as an integral system
problem, evaluating competence from an integrated perspective rather than through isolated metrics.
This comprehensive approach focuses on supporting and improving human judgment, meaningfully
triangulating assessment information, and constructing fairness, credibility, and defensibility within a
systemic framework. (Schuwirth, 2020)
The transformation in assessment practices in medical education mirrors the rapid changes in society,
education, and healthcare, suggesting that the field may continue to evolve. The journey from viewing
assessment through a reductionist lens to embracing it as a holistic, system-wide challenge highlights
the dynamic nature of medical education and the continuous quest for methods that not only evaluate
but also enhance learning and competence in healthcare professionals.
In the evolving landscape of education and research, the construction of questions stands as a
cornerstone for reliable and valid assessments.The meticulous crafting of these questions influences
not only the measurement of educational achievements and survey outcomes but also the integrity of
data-driven decisions across various fields. Historically, the journey toward refining question quality has
been marked by continuous efforts to adhere to guidelines for good assessment. These guidelines
emphasize clarity, fairness, relevance, and the avoidance of bias, ensuring that each question
accurately gauges the intended construct without prejudice or ambiguity.
1

Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs) are widely utilized in medical education for assessment purposes,
surpassing other methods like short answer questions, oral exams, and written reports in frequency of
use for summative evaluations faced by medical students. Their preference is largely due to the
efficiency with which they can cover a wide range of knowledge, facilitate the establishment of
standards, and generate statistical data critical for maintaining the quality of institutional question
databases. However, MCQs often face criticism for not adequately promoting critical thinking or
assessing attitudes, behaviors, or clinical skill application, and for not addressing gender and cultural
biases in responses. Consequently, standard MCQs are rarely linked with transformative learning
experiences. Nevertheless, a diverse array of MCQ formats exists that can assess a spectrum of
competencies, including knowledge, skills, attitudes, judgment, and behaviors, particularly when the
questions are framed within specific contexts. ( Douthit, 2021) Of paramount importance is also the
quest for quality in question construction. The National Board of Medical Examiners (NBME) produced a
very comprehensive guide on item writing: “NBME Item-Writing Guide” which is one of the most
valuable tools to empower faculties in developing high quality questions. Despite the continuous effort
to control quality, most methodologies for creating and evaluating questions rely heavily on expert
judgment and manual review, a process that is time and man-work consuming and not completely
devoid of subtle flaws. Issues such as ambiguity in question wording, cultural or linguistic biases, and
inconsistencies across test items have persisted, threatening the validity and reliability of assessments.
These imperfections can lead to misinterpretations of a respondent's knowledge or opinions, ultimately
affecting educational outcomes, research findings, and policy decisions.
The significance of these challenges is not a modern revelation. Since the inception of standardized
testing and survey methodologies, educators, researchers, and policymakers have grappled with the
complexities of question construction. In the early 20th century, as standardized testing began to take
shape, the focus was primarily on the practicality and scalability of assessments. However, as the
century progressed, the emphasis shifted towards the nuanced aspects of test construction, including
the psychometric properties of test items and the ethical considerations of testing practices. Despite
advancements in assessment theory and practice, the enduring struggle to balance efficiency, accuracy,
and fairness in question construction has remained a critical concern.

MATERIAL AND METHODS
Study dates
The study was conducted at the School of Medicine, University of Minho, Braga, Portugal, from October
2022 to February 2024.
Study Design
The study was developed in 4 steps
First step – question database selection, randomization and validation.
In order to address our aims, we needed a solid medical question database. We used a database
created by Silva in 2020 (Silva,2020), which looked at 1150 questions from Portuguese NSEs from
2009 to 2018 (1000 items) and RNAE pilot exam (150) that were previously studied and submitted to a
manual identification of item writing flaws. We selected a smaller sample through a process of stratified
random sampling in order to assure representation of all the IWF, which the final result of a group of
106 question.
Manual IWF evaluation
We performed a manual validation of the selected sample in order to validate the new database,
concerning agreement with the previously detected IWF in criteria’s for manual analysis of the IFWs
such as the quality rating calculation. (Rush, 2016; Jozefowicz, 2002)
Second step: Selection of AI model and evaluation of the intrinsic knowledge about question
construction and item writing flaw detection.
AI Large language models
In order to assess the performance of a AI model we used ChatGPT and GPT-4, developed by OpenAI
(https://openai.com). Language models are designed to process and generate text that mirrors human-
like conversation. They achieve this by predicting what next word (token) should come next given an
input, allowing them to generate coherent, contextually relevant responses. ChatGPT, an earlier version,
has been widely used in a variety of applications due to its ability to generate coherent and contextually
6

relevant responses. GPT-4, is similar in design but much larger in scale, meaning it has been trained on
more data and can understand and generate text with even greater accuracy.
To test the intrinsic knowledge on the subject, we asked directly if GPT-4 was familiar with the concept
of “item writing flaws detection in A-type (One-best-answer item) MCQs”, and he described the concept
of writing questions and of item writing detection. (APPENDIX 1)
Third step: Evaluate each individual question from our 106 items database for the presence of 14
different IWF: “Longest option is correct answer”; “Grammatical clues or inconsistencies between stem
and distractors”; ”Implausible distractors”; “Mutually exclusive distractors”; “Use of absolute terms”;
“Use of all of the above”; “Awkward stem structure”; ”Extraneous or misleading information in the
stem”; “Negative stem”; “Response options are a series of true/false statements”; “Use of none of the
above”; “Complex or K-type items”; “Vague or generalizing terms” and “Unfocused question”. In
order to perform this task we established a strategy of prompt development for each one of the IFW.
(APPENDIX 2)
Fourth step – Compare IWF detection in both models.
Number of identified IWF
Items were considered generally flawed or non-flawed based on the presence of at least one IWF or the
total absence of IWFs, respectively.
Statistics and Analysis
Given that we are dealing with a binary outcome (e.g., positive/negative), to check if there was a
systematic difference between the results obtained from each method, McNemar’s Chi squared test
was performed in each IWFs analysis (statistically significant result with P < 0.05). To check the degree
of agreement between both tests, we calculated Cohen’s kappa and the standard error of kappa for
each IWF analysis (with kappa ranging from 0 to 1, with zero meaning that the agreement is no better
than a result obtained by chance and one meaning that there is a perfect agreement between the tests.
(Royal, 2017) For interpretation, we used the scale below, proposed by Landis et al.

DISCUSSION
With our work we purposed to present a comprehensive analysis of the most important IWF and to
compare the detection capabilities of GPT-4 and human evaluators across multiple IWF categories. Our
starting point was the database already assessed for IWF by Silva et al.
Our starting point was a database extensively analyzed concerning quality in item generation.
The comprehensive dataset presented delineates the outcomes of a rigorous comparative analysis
between GPT-4's automated assessment capabilities and human manual detection in identifying various
Item Writing Flaws (IWFs). This side-by-side evaluation employed two statistical methodologies:
McNemar’s Chi-squared test and Cohen's Kappa analysis. These methods were specifically chosen to
quantify the systematic effects and to measure the level of agreement, respectively, between the two
detection modalities.
We are going to review briefly the main findings across the several IWFs:
 Longest Option Is Correct Answer: The process of automatically detecting questions where
the correct answer is the longest option can be affected or changed by the nature of the
required answer. This issue often arises when the person creating the question feels compelled
to ensure the answer is beyond dispute, leading to a longer option compared to the others.
Additionally, the specific threshold at which the system identifies an answer as "the longest"
plays a role in this identification process. (Rush,2016) In our work both the AI and human
methods show high sensitivity and specificity, indicating a reliable identification of this flaw. The
moderate Cohen's Kappa suggests that while there is agreement, there is still room for
improvement. It is noteworthy that most of the answer from GTP-4 have a small justification of
the classification, and the framework of the rationale is that there shouldn’t be stems that are
longer than the average, neither the correct nor the others. This contributes for the number of
false positive comparing with the manual review data.
 Grammatical Clues or Inconsistencies: The use of nongrammatically correct options
provide cues to the student who easily eliminates distracters that do not flow grammatically
with the stem, influencing the performance of in the item irrespectively of assessment
objectives. The high specificity and moderate sensitivity, along with a moderate Cohen's Kappa,
suggest that while human evaluators are reliably ruling out items without issues, they are
moderately successful in identifying items with grammatical flaws. GTP-4 evaluation is just
moderately compliant with the human one.
17

 Implausible Distractors: This IWF can impact the performance of the test taker,
automatically avoiding the stem with it, and with a potential positive impact on mean scores.
The analysis reveals a fair level of specificity but low sensitivity, indicating a moderate degree of
detection effectiveness. The low positive predictive value suggests many distractors may be
missed, and the slight agreement indicated by Cohen's Kappa implies only some alignment in
detection. The detection of an implausible distractor is somehow directly linked with context
knowledge of the observer.CGT-4 has general medical knowledge built in, as several studies
already described the potential have good performance in medical examinations. (Sallam,
2024; Meo, 2023)
 Mutually Exclusive Distractors: The results indicate higher reliability in ruling out non-
mutually exclusive distractors, with a fair level of specificity. The negative Cohen's Kappa,
however, points to a fair level of disagreement, which is concerning. Just as in the previous
IWF, mutually exclusive distractors can change the tester performance. GPT-4 performed in a
less efficient way with low sensitivity score.
 Use of Absolute Terms: The use of absolute terms is recognized by experienced students as
indicator of wrong answers and is often immediately dismissed as a plausible distractor. This is
because absolute terms are frequently found in incorrect statements. In our analysis the results
do not show a statistically significant difference in detection capability. The lack of sensitivity
and the negative Cohen's Kappa highlight a serious inconsistency in detecting this flaw. GPT-4
failed to identify the use of absolute terms.
 Awkward Stem Structure: The methods appear ineffective in identifying this flaw, as
indicated by zero sensitivity and a negative Cohen's Kappa value. This suggests that neither
method is reliable for detecting awkward stem structures.
 Extraneous or Misleading Information: Both GPT-4 and human evaluators fail to
effectively identify this flaw, with zero sensitivity and a negligible Cohen's Kappa value. This
indicates an ineffective detection method for this type of IWF.
 Negative Stem: The reported perfect scores across all metrics are highly unusual in practical
scenarios and could suggest an oversimplification in the test design or data issues. Absolute
agreement in real-world conditions is rare, so these results warrant a closer examination of
methodology.
18

 Response Options as True/False Statements: The low sensitivity and specificity, coupled
with a negative Cohen's Kappa, imply a poor detection capability for this flaw. The high PPV is
contradicted by other metrics, suggesting a potential anomaly in the data.
 Vague or Generalizing Terms: No significant difference is detected between the methods,
but the low sensitivity and PPV show ineffectiveness in identifying this flaw. The negative
Cohen's Kappa suggests slight disagreement between methods.
 Unfocused Question: Extremely low sensitivity and PPV, along with a negative Cohen's
Kappa, indicate very poor performance in detecting this flaw by both methods.
In general, McNemar’s test, which is apt for paired nominal data, helped us to discern whether
the differences in detection rates between GPT-4 and human raters were statistically significant. This
was particularly important in identifying asymmetries where one method may detect an IWF more
frequently than the other. The resulting Chi-square values, some exceeding the threshold of statistical
significance (e.g., P value <0.0001 for "Negative stem"), illuminated instances of substantial divergence
or concurrence in the performance of GPT-4 and human raters.
Meanwhile, Cohen's Kappa analysis offered a nuanced view of the agreement between the two
evaluators, adjusting for the agreement that could occur by chance. This analysis ranged from
indicating a moderate level of agreement (Kappa value of 0.439 for "Longest option is correct answer")
to revealing substantial disagreement (Kappa value of -0.388 for "Unfocused question"). These Kappa
values, along with their respective standard errors, provided a robust measure of inter-rater reliability,
demonstrating areas of consistency and disparity.
It is noteworthy that the combination of McNemar’s Chi-squared test and Cohen's Kappa analysis
provided a holistic view of the comparative effectiveness of GPT-4 and human detection of IWFs. While
high specificity and NPV across several IWF categories suggested a reliable exclusion of non-flaws, the
varying sensitivity and PPV highlighted the challenges in flaw detection. The negative Cohen’s Kappa
values in certain categories underscored the need for further investigation to reconcile the differences
between GPT-4 and human evaluators and to enhance the accuracy of IWF detection methodologies.
The absence of detection for certain IWFs such as "Use of none of the above" and “Complex or K-type
items” could indicated that both GPT-4 and human raters were unable to identify any instances of these
flaws, however the database was manual validated for the absence of those IWF.
Despite expecting a better performance of GPT-4 both in the systematic effect of detecting IWF and in
degree of agreement, the use of a novel tool has limitations and challenges that must be addressed.
These results support the proof of concept that AI tools can identify IFW.

CONCLUSIONS
This study compares the detection of various IWFs by an AI model, GPT-4, against human manual
analysis, which is considered the gold standard. The use of McNemar’s Chi-squared test and Cohen's
Kappa provides a robust framework for evaluating the agreement and systematic differences between
the two detection methods.
The results portray a mixed performance of GPT-4 and human evaluators in detecting various IWFs.
While some areas show promise, such as the detection of the longest option being correct, other areas,
including the detection of vague terms and unfocused questions, highlight significant weaknesses.
In conclusion, this analysis underscores the importance of continuous improvement in AI models for
IWF detection and the need for human evaluators to receive ongoing training to maintain high standards
in item writing and evaluation. The discrepancies between AI and human detection also highlight the
complexity of IWF detection and the need for multifaceted approaches to training and evaluation.

Identifying and addressing these flaws is crucial for creating high-quality assessments that accurately
measure what they are intended to.
Some common item writing flaws in MCQs include:
1. **Use of Absolutes**: Words like "always," "never," "all," or "none" can often make an option
obviously right or wrong.
2. **Ambiguity**: Poorly worded questions or answers that can be interpreted in multiple ways, leading
to confusion.
3. **Grammatical Cues**: Inconsistencies in grammar or phrasing between the stem and the options,
which can inadvertently lead to the correct answer.
4. **Test-wise Cues**: Patterns in the options or the position of the correct answer that test-wise
students can exploit.
25

5. **Use of "All of the Above" or "None of the Above"**: These can be problematic because they may
not always test the student's knowledge accurately. For instance, if a student knows that two options
are correct, they can choose "All of the above" without knowing about the other options.
6. **Overly Specific or General Options**: Options that are significantly more detailed or more general
than others can stand out as either obviously correct or incorrect.
7. **Negative Wording**: Double negatives or questions asking for the "not correct" or "least likely"
option can be confusing and mislead students.
8. **Trivia Questions**: Focusing on trivial facts rather than concepts or critical thinking.
9. **Convergence Strategy**: Options that are too similar or that overlap significantly, allowing test-
takers to deduce the correct answer through elimination.
10. **Implausible Distractors**: Incorrect options (distractors) that are obviously wrong, which do not
effectively test the student's knowledge.
Identifying these flaws requires a thorough review of the questions, an understanding of the content
being tested, and an awareness of common pitfalls in question design. It's also beneficial to have a
clear objective for what each question is intended to measure and to ensure that the question and its
options align with that objective.
If you have specific MCQs you'd like to discuss or need advice on identifying or correcting item writing
flaws, feel free to share more details!”
